Print, Letters, and Early Reading

k.ela.print-and-early-reading · Grade K ela · validated

A five-day, adult-supported introduction to book handling, print direction, words and sentences, letter sounds, and early word reading.

Standards

K.RF.1.a, K.RF.1.b, K.RF.1.c, K.RF.2.c, K.RF.2.d, K.RF.3.a, K.RF.3.b, K.RF.3.c, K.RF.3.d

Materials

  • A favorite picture book with clear print (alternatives: a library book, a short printed poem)
  • Letter cards (alternatives: letters written on paper squares, magnetic letters)
  • A pointer or finger puppet (alternatives: a clean finger)
  • Paper and a thick pencil or crayon (alternatives: a small whiteboard and marker)

Day 1: Books Have Parts and Print Has a Job

Objective: I can show where a book starts and where the words are.

Adult setup: Place the book face down and keep the letter cards out of sight until practice. Sit beside the learner so both can point to the same page.

Text recommendation: Use a familiar picture book with a clear front cover, title, author name, and one short line of print on each page.

Warm-up

  1. Invite the learner to choose the front of the book.
  2. Name the cover, pages, and words together.

Explicit model

  1. Model opening a book at the front and turning pages one at a time.
  2. Point beneath a line of print and explain that readers begin on this side and move across the page.

Guided practice

  1. Take turns showing the cover, title, and a page with words.
  2. Read one short line while the learner moves the pointer underneath from left to right.
  3. Gently return to the starting side when the pointer moves the other way.

Independent practice

  1. Complete three short on-screen print-concept items without answer-revealing hints.
  2. Then show an adult where to begin reading on a new page.

Templates: k.rf.1.a.book-parts, k.rf.1.b.print-direction

Extension

  1. Let the learner be the book helper and tell an adult what each book part is for.

Reteach

  1. Use only the cover and one inside page.
  2. Give a choice of two places to start, then model and repeat the correct routine.

Mastery evidence

  • Identifies the front cover and a page containing print.
  • Tracks one short line from left to right with a finger or pointer.

Day 2: Words Make Sentences

Objective: I can find a word and tell where a sentence begins and ends.

Adult setup: Write one short sentence in large print, such as Sam sat. Prepare three paper strips: one word, two words, and the full sentence.

Text recommendation: Choose a repetitive read-aloud so the learner can notice the same printed sentence more than once.

Warm-up

  1. Reread one line from Day 1 while pointing under each word.
  2. Clap once for each word in the line.

Explicit model

  1. Model leaving a small space between written words.
  2. Point to the capital letter at the beginning and the punctuation at the end of the sample sentence.

Guided practice

  1. Build the sample sentence from word strips together.
  2. Ask the learner to point to one word, then the first and last parts of the sentence.
  3. Read the completed sentence together while sweeping under each word.

Independent practice

  1. Complete three on-screen items about print direction, words, and sentences.
  2. Ask the learner to explain one choice using pointing or speech.

Templates: k.rf.1.b.print-direction, k.rf.1.c.words-and-sentences

Extension

  1. Cut apart a familiar three-word sentence and rebuild it in order.

Reteach

  1. Use a two-word sentence and cover all but one word at a time.
  2. Read, point, and rebuild before adding another word.

Mastery evidence

  • Distinguishes a word from a whole sentence in two examples.
  • Tracks the words in one familiar short sentence in order.

Day 3: Letters Connect to Sounds

Objective: I can name a letter and connect it to a sound in a familiar word.

Adult setup: Choose four known letter cards and four picture or object examples. Use only reviewed recordings or an adult's natural voice for isolated phonemes.

Text recommendation: Choose an alphabet book that gives one concrete picture for each letter; pause for only the letters the learner is ready to study.

Warm-up

  1. Sing or say the names of the four chosen letters.
  2. Invite the learner to match one card to its picture.

Explicit model

  1. Name one letter, point to it, and say the first sound in a familiar word.
  2. Explain that the picture word begins with that sound without asking the learner to read it.

Guided practice

  1. Sort two letter cards at a time with adult speech support.
  2. Ask which letter matches the first sound in each pictured word.
  3. Repeat a correct match with the letter name and a word example.

Independent practice

  1. Complete three reviewed initial-sound and letter-sound items without an answer-revealing hint.
  2. Keep directions spoken, but do not pronounce an assessed answer choice.

Templates: k.rf.2.d.initial-sound, k.rf.3.a.letter-sounds

Extension

  1. Find one chosen letter in the picture book and name it.

Reteach

  1. Work with one letter and two picture choices.
  2. Use the same concrete word several times before introducing another letter.

Mastery evidence

  • Names at least three studied letters.
  • Matches each of those letters to a familiar beginning-sound example with appropriate support recorded.

Day 4: Slide Sounds Into a Word

Objective: I can use letter sounds to read a simple three-sound word.

Adult setup: Prepare letter cards for two short consonant-vowel-consonant words such as map and sat. Do not use synthesized isolated phonemes; use reviewed cues or adult modeling.

Text recommendation: Use a short controlled-text sentence only after the learner has practiced the matching word with letter cards.

Warm-up

  1. Review two letter-sound matches from Day 3.
  2. Place three letter cards in a row and name the letters together.

Explicit model

  1. Touch each letter card, say its sound, then slide a finger under the whole word.
  2. Model blending the sounds into one spoken word and matching it to a picture.

Guided practice

  1. Build one word together, touching each card in order.
  2. Name the vowel letter and connect it to its short sound in the word.
  3. Blend the word and use a picture choice to check it.

Independent practice

  1. Complete three reviewed blending, medial-sound, and vowel-spelling items without an answer-revealing hint.
  2. Record whether the learner blended independently or used adult support.

Templates: k.rf.2.c.blending-segmenting, k.rf.2.d.medial-vowel, k.rf.3.a.letter-sounds, k.rf.3.b.vowel-sounds

Extension

  1. Build a new CVC word by changing one letter and draw a quick picture for it.

Reteach

  1. Return to two letter cards and blend them slowly.
  2. Add the final card only after the learner can repeat the first two sounds.

Mastery evidence

  • Blends two practiced CVC words with letter cards.
  • Completes at least two independent items without answer-revealing audio.

Day 5: Read and Notice Familiar Words

Objective: I can use print skills, letter sounds, and familiar words while reading a short controlled sentence.

Adult setup: Prepare one short controlled sentence and the matching word cards. Plan a short movement or drawing break between the model and independent work.

Text recommendation: Choose a decodable or controlled sentence that uses the practiced sound pattern and one familiar high-frequency word.

Warm-up

  1. Review the book-start routine and point under a familiar short sentence.
  2. Name one known letter and one known word.

Explicit model

  1. Model starting at the correct side, tracking each word, blending a regular word, and recognizing a high-frequency word by sight.
  2. Compare one similar word pair, such as map and mop, and identify the different middle letters and sounds.

Guided practice

  1. Read the controlled sentence together twice.
  2. Pause before one word for the learner to use a letter-sound or sight-word strategy.
  3. Compare a similar word pair and point to the letter and sound that change.

Independent practice

  1. Complete four mixed print and early-reading items without answer-revealing hints.
  2. Offer a break before the final item if needed.

Templates: k.rf.1.a.book-parts, k.rf.1.b.print-direction, k.rf.1.c.words-and-sentences, k.rf.2.c.blending-segmenting, k.rf.2.d.medial-vowel, k.rf.3.a.letter-sounds, k.rf.3.b.vowel-sounds, k.rf.3.c.high-frequency-words, k.rf.3.d.similar-words

Extension

  1. Make a mini page with one drawing and one dictated or copied controlled sentence.

Reteach

  1. Choose one success from the week and repeat the concrete model.
  2. Reduce the sentence to one word or one sound-to-letter match, then build back up after success.

Mastery evidence

  • Uses correct book and print direction routines.
  • Shows a letter-sound, word-building, or familiar-word strategy on a controlled sentence.
  • Treat this as instructional evidence; repeated independent practice is still required for application mastery.

Accessibility and accommodations

  • Read directions aloud and permit replay without penalty.
  • Use large high-contrast print and tactile letter cards when helpful.
  • Accept pointing, eye gaze, signing, or spoken response when handwriting is not being assessed.
  • Provide extra wait time and teach fewer letter-sound relationships at once without treating the accommodation as a failure.

Audio supported: Yes; requires color: No; reduced-motion safe: Yes.

Short Vowels and CVC Sound Work

k.ela.short-vowels-and-cvc-sounds · Grade K ela · validated

A five-day, adult-supported sequence for hearing short vowels, isolating phonemes, blending and segmenting consonant-vowel-consonant words, changing sounds, and spelling simple words.

Standards

K.L.2.c, K.RF.2.c, K.RF.2.d, K.RF.2.e, K.RF.3.a, K.RF.3.b

Materials

  • Lowercase letter cards for common consonants and vowels (alternatives: letters written on paper squares, magnetic letters)
  • Three sound boxes and counters (alternatives: three circles drawn on paper and small blocks)
  • Picture cards or familiar objects for simple CVC words (alternatives: adult-drawn pictures, objects found around the home)
  • Paper and a thick pencil or crayon (alternatives: a small whiteboard and marker)

Day 1: Hear Short A and Short I

Objective: I can hear the middle sound in a simple word and tell whether it is short a or short i.

Adult setup: Prepare pictures or objects for map, cat, fish, and pig plus the lowercase a and i cards. Use an adult's natural voice or a reviewed phoneme recording for isolated sounds; ordinary text-to-speech must not model the assessed phoneme.

Text recommendation: Choose an alphabet or rhyming book with several clear short-a and short-i words; use the book for listening, not as an independent decoding demand.

Warm-up

  1. Say two familiar words that begin with the same sound and notice the first sound together.
  2. Move one counter for the first, middle, and last sounds in map.

Explicit model

  1. Stretch map without adding an extra vowel after either consonant and identify the middle sound.
  2. Place the a card in the middle sound box, then repeat with fish and the i card.
  3. Contrast map and mip as spoken forms so the learner hears that changing the vowel changes the word.

Guided practice

  1. Sort pictured words by the short-a or short-i middle sound.
  2. For each word, move three counters while saying the sounds with the learner.
  3. Ask the learner to point to the vowel card and explain the choice by repeating the middle sound.

Independent practice

  1. Complete four on-screen initial-, medial-, and vowel-sound checks without answer-revealing speech.
  2. Then say and segment one pictured word for the adult; the on-screen checks support but do not replace this oral evidence.

Templates: k.rf.2.d.initial-sound, k.rf.2.d.medial-vowel, k.rf.3.b.vowel-sounds

Extension

  1. Find or draw one more short-a word and one more short-i word, then identify each middle sound.

Reteach

  1. Use only two pictured words with the same beginning and ending, such as map and mip.
  2. Say each word slowly, point to the middle box, and let the learner imitate before choosing a vowel card.

Mastery evidence

  • Correctly isolates the medial sound in at least three CVC examples with adult-recorded support level.
  • Distinguishes short a from short i in at least three of four spoken examples.

Day 2: Hear Short O, Short E, and Short U

Objective: I can listen for short o, short e, and short u in the middle of simple words.

Adult setup: Prepare pictures or objects for sun, cup, dog, log, hen, and net plus lowercase o, e, and u cards. Preview every word and replace any picture the learner cannot readily name.

Text recommendation: Choose a playful read-aloud containing familiar CVC words; pause to notice only a few target words so meaning remains central.

Warm-up

  1. Review one short-a and one short-i example from Day 1.
  2. Say sun, dog, and hen while the learner taps once for each sound.

Explicit model

  1. Model stretching sun and listening in the middle, then place u in the middle sound box.
  2. Repeat with dog and hen, keeping each vowel sound brief and natural.
  3. Explain that the vowel is the middle letter in these three-sound words.

Guided practice

  1. Sort six pictures under o, e, or u.
  2. Check each choice by segmenting the word into three sounds together.
  3. Mix in one short-a and one short-i picture for cumulative comparison.

Independent practice

  1. Complete four on-screen medial-vowel, final-sound, and vowel-spelling checks.
  2. Keep assessed choices silent; use an adult's natural voice only for the target word when the item requires listening.

Templates: k.rf.2.d.medial-vowel, k.rf.2.d.final-sound, k.rf.3.b.vowel-sounds

Extension

  1. Make five vowel headings and place one practiced picture under each heading.

Reteach

  1. Contrast only two vowels at a time using words with matching consonants when possible.
  2. Let the learner watch the adult's mouth, repeat the word, and touch the middle sound box before choosing.

Mastery evidence

  • Sorts familiar CVC words by two or more target short-vowel sounds.
  • Isolates the middle sound rather than naming a picture or guessing from its first letter.

Day 3: Stretch, Tap, and Blend

Objective: I can separate three sounds in a CVC word and blend them back into the word.

Adult setup: Prepare three sound boxes, counters, and letter cards for map, sit, sun, and log. Plan to accept pointing or counter movement when speech production is difficult, while still checking sound awareness separately from articulation.

Text recommendation: Use a short oral wordplay rhyme with clear one-syllable words; this day's primary text is spoken language rather than connected print.

Warm-up

  1. Push one counter for each sound in a practiced word.
  2. Sweep a finger under the counters and say the whole word without pausing.

Explicit model

  1. Say map, stretch it into three phonemes, and move one counter per sound.
  2. Replace each counter with its lowercase letter while repeating the same sound sequence.
  3. Slide a finger under the letters and blend the sounds into map.

Guided practice

  1. Segment and blend three words together using counters before letters.
  2. Ask which sound is first, middle, and last in different examples.
  3. Have the learner choose the matching lowercase letters after the oral sound work succeeds.

Independent practice

  1. Complete five on-screen blending, medial-vowel, and letter-sound checks without answer-revealing narration.
  2. Orally segment and blend one new adult-selected CVC word; record whether it was independent, prompted, or modeled.

Templates: k.rf.2.c.blending-segmenting, k.rf.2.d.medial-vowel, k.rf.3.a.letter-sounds

Extension

  1. Draw a three-box sound train and teach an adult how to ride each sound into the whole word.

Reteach

  1. Blend two parts first by holding the first two sounds close together, then add the final sound.
  2. Return to counters without print if letter naming competes with listening.

Mastery evidence

  • Segments at least two CVC words into three ordered phonemes using voice, counters, or another accessible response.
  • Blends at least two modeled sound sequences into recognizable words.

Day 4: Change a Sound and Spell a Word

Objective: I can change one sound in a CVC word and write the letters that match the sounds.

Adult setup: Prepare letter cards that can build map, mop, top, tip, and sit. Provide pencil support or movable letters so handwriting effort does not hide phoneme knowledge.

Text recommendation: Choose a simple word-family poem for an adult read-aloud, then compare its rhyming words by sound and letter.

Warm-up

  1. Build map with letter cards and touch each sound.
  2. Ask which card is in the middle and which sound it represents.

Explicit model

  1. Change map to mop by replacing a with o and say both words.
  2. Change mop to top by replacing the first letter and identify the changed phoneme.
  3. Segment top and write one letter for each sound, saying the sound before writing its letter.

Guided practice

  1. Make a short word chain by changing only one sound at a time.
  2. Before moving a card, ask whether the changed sound is first, middle, or last.
  3. Dictate one familiar CVC word for the learner to build or write, then read it back together.

Independent practice

  1. Complete five on-screen sound-substitution, sound-position, letter-sound, and spelling checks.
  2. Spell one adult-dictated CVC word with movable letters or writing without showing the answer first.

Templates: k.rf.2.e.sound-substitution, k.rf.2.d.medial-vowel, k.rf.3.a.letter-sounds, k.l.2.c.cvc-spelling

Extension

  1. Create a two-step word chain and challenge an adult to name the sound that changed each time.

Reteach

  1. Keep two letters fixed and offer only two cards for the changing position.
  2. Say both resulting words and move a counter for the changed sound before returning to letters.

Mastery evidence

  • Adds or substitutes one phoneme to make a new spoken CVC word.
  • Represents most sounds in at least two familiar CVC words with plausible letters.

Day 5: Use Every Sound in a CVC Word

Objective: I can listen, segment, blend, change, and spell simple words with short vowels.

Adult setup: Prepare six previously practiced pictures and letter cards plus one new regular CVC word. Choose target words whose pronunciations are familiar in the learner's home dialect and record support without penalizing dialect difference.

Text recommendation: Use a very short adult-checked controlled sentence containing only practiced sound patterns and familiar words; inspect every word before presenting it.

Warm-up

  1. Review all five lowercase vowel cards and say one familiar short-vowel word for each.
  2. Let the learner choose two words to segment with counters.

Explicit model

  1. Model a full routine with one word: listen, repeat, segment, choose letters, blend, and check meaning.
  2. Show how to return to the sound boxes when the spelling or blended word does not match.

Guided practice

  1. Complete the full routine together for two words with different short vowels.
  2. Read the adult-checked controlled sentence together and locate a practiced CVC word.
  3. Change one sound in that word and decide whether the result is a real word.

Independent practice

  1. Complete six mixed on-screen checks without answer-revealing narration.
  2. Independently segment, build, and blend one familiar word; use the on-screen score only as supporting evidence.

Templates: k.rf.2.c.blending-segmenting, k.rf.2.d.medial-vowel, k.rf.2.e.sound-substitution, k.rf.3.a.letter-sounds, k.rf.3.b.vowel-sounds, k.l.2.c.cvc-spelling

Extension

  1. Make a tiny word book with one practiced word and picture per page, then read it to an adult.

Reteach

  1. Choose the strongest word from the week and complete one step at a time with counters and letters.
  2. Remove answer choices, reduce the number of sounds being compared, and rebuild only after a successful oral response.

Mastery evidence

  • Segments and blends a familiar CVC word without an answer choice supplying the word.
  • Selects or writes a plausible letter for each phoneme in a familiar CVC word.
  • Treats digital question results as supporting checks rather than proof of independent oral phoneme performance.

Accessibility and accommodations

  • Read directions aloud but keep assessed phoneme and answer choices from being revealed by narration.
  • Use an adult's natural voice or reviewed isolated-phoneme recording; ordinary text-to-speech is not a phoneme model.
  • Accept counters, pointing, eye gaze, signing, or letter selection when speech or handwriting is not the assessed skill.
  • Teach fewer sound contrasts at once, allow extra processing time, and record support level without treating an accommodation as failure.

Audio supported: Yes; requires color: No; reduced-motion safe: Yes.

Counting and Quantities

k.math.counting-and-quantities · Grade K math · reviewed

A five-day sequence for counting to 100 by ones and tens, counting on from a given number, and connecting written numerals to quantities.

Standards

K.CC.A.1, K.CC.A.2, K.CC.A.3

Materials

  • Twenty small counters (alternatives: buttons, blocks, dry beans)
  • Numeral cards from 0 through 20 (alternatives: numbers written on scrap paper)
  • A hundred chart (alternatives: a hand-written grid showing 1 through 100)
  • Ten bundles with ten craft sticks each (alternatives: ten groups of ten counters, ten interlocking-cube trains of ten)
  • Paper and a thick pencil or crayon (alternatives: small whiteboard and marker)
  • Two ten-frames (alternatives: two hand-drawn grids with ten boxes each)

Day 1: Count to 100 by Ones

Objective: I can say the counting sequence to 100 by ones.

Adult setup: Place the hundred chart where both learners can point to it. Mark the decade-ending numbers 10, 20, 30, and so on with removable notes or small objects.

Text recommendation: Choose a short counting book from home or the library that moves beyond 20; pause at page changes so the learner can predict the next number.

Warm-up

  1. Count aloud together from 1 to 20.
  2. Ask what number comes next and continue together to 30.

Explicit model

  1. Point to each number on the hundred chart while counting from 1 to 30.
  2. Pause at 29 and model that the next decade begins with 30.
  3. Continue by pointing out the repeating 1-through-9 pattern after each decade number.

Guided practice

  1. Take turns leading one row of the hundred chart at a time.
  2. Pause before 40, 50, 60, 70, 80, 90, and 100 so the learner can supply the next number.
  3. Repeat any difficult transition, then reconnect it to the full sequence.

Independent practice

  1. Complete five on-screen counting-order questions without an answer-revealing hint.
  2. Treat these small-range questions as a check of the early sequence, then separately ask the learner to count aloud to 100.

Templates: k.cc.a.1.count-from-one, k.cc.a.1.missing-in-order, k.cc.a.1.order-small-set

Extension

  1. Begin at a decade number such as 40 and continue by ones to the next decade.
  2. Find and describe a pattern in one column of the hundred chart.

Reteach

  1. Cover all but one row of the hundred chart.
  2. Count that group of ten together, then reveal the next row.
  3. Practice only one difficult decade transition before reconnecting it to a longer count.

Mastery evidence

  • Counts aloud to 100 by ones with no more than two self-corrected omissions or sequence errors.
  • Successfully continues through at least three different decade transitions.
  • Answers at least four of five small-range independent items correctly without an answer-revealing hint; these items alone do not demonstrate the full standard.

Day 2: Count to 100 by Tens

Objective: I can say the counting sequence to 100 by tens and connect each count to one group of ten.

Adult setup: Lay out ten bundles or groups of ten in a row. Keep the hundred chart visible and point out its decade-ending column.

Text recommendation: Choose a counting text, song transcript, or classroom chart that shows groups of ten; point to each printed decade number as it is read aloud.

Warm-up

  1. Count from 1 to 20 by ones.
  2. Circle or point to 10 and 20 on the hundred chart.

Explicit model

  1. Touch one bundle and say 10, then touch the next bundle and say 20.
  2. Continue through 100, explaining that each new bundle adds ten more.
  3. Point to the matching decade number on the hundred chart after touching each bundle.

Guided practice

  1. Take turns adding one bundle and naming the new total by tens.
  2. Mix the decade numeral cards and match each card to the correct number of bundles.
  3. Count by tens from 10 to 100 once without adult speech support.

Independent practice

  1. Complete four on-screen counting-order questions to reinforce that a fixed counting sequence has a next number.
  2. Then count the ten bundles by tens while an adult records the oral performance separately.

Templates: k.cc.a.1.count-from-one, k.cc.a.1.count-from-given, k.cc.a.1.missing-in-order

Extension

  1. Start at 30 and count by tens to 100.
  2. Explain how counting ten bundles by tens is different from counting ten individual objects by ones.

Reteach

  1. Use only three bundles and the cards 10, 20, and 30.
  2. Match one spoken count to each whole bundle.
  3. Add one bundle at a time until the learner can continue the decade sequence.

Mastery evidence

  • Counts aloud by tens from 10 through 100 while touching one group for each number.
  • Matches at least five decade numerals to the corresponding number of ten-bundles.
  • Record the oral and concrete performance because the current on-screen item bank does not independently assess counting by tens.

Day 3: Start Anywhere and Count On

Objective: I can begin with a number I am given and count forward.

Adult setup: Arrange numeral cards 1 through 20 in a visible number path. Keep five counters nearby for marking jumps on the path.

Text recommendation: Choose a familiar rhyme or poem with a number sequence and invite the learner to continue the sequence after you pause.

Warm-up

  1. Count from 1 to 20 together.
  2. Cover the cards before 6, point to 6, and count forward from there.

Explicit model

  1. Point to 8 and say, I already know there are eight, so I start at eight.
  2. Move a counter to 9, 10, and 11 while saying each next number.
  3. Explain that counting on does not require returning to 1.

Guided practice

  1. Take turns choosing a starting card from 3 through 15.
  2. Count forward three numbers while moving a counter along the path.
  3. Ask, What number comes next, and how do you know?

Independent practice

  1. Complete five on-screen count-on questions without an answer-revealing hint.
  2. Use the number path only when needed, then try the final item without it.

Templates: k.cc.a.2.after-small-number, k.cc.a.2.after-teen, k.cc.a.2.missing-next, k.cc.a.2.count-on-two

Extension

  1. Choose a starting number between 11 and 19 and count forward five numbers.
  2. Try counting on with the number path turned face down.

Reteach

  1. Use cards 1 through 10 only.
  2. Have the learner place a finger on the starting number before speaking.
  3. Move one space for each next number and stop after two moves.

Mastery evidence

  • Counts forward at least five numbers from three different starting numbers within 20.
  • Explains that the given number is the starting number.
  • Answers at least four of five independent items correctly without an answer-revealing hint.

Day 4: Numbers Show How Many

Objective: I can write a numeral from 0 through 20 and match it to a group with that many objects.

Adult setup: Mix numeral cards 0 through 20. Place paper, a writing tool, counters, and ten-frames on the work surface.

Text recommendation: Choose a numeral or counting book with clear printed digits and compare one printed numeral with the matching numeral card.

Warm-up

  1. Show numeral cards 0 through 10 in mixed order for the learner to name.
  2. Place the cards in order together.

Explicit model

  1. Choose the numeral 6 and say its name.
  2. Place six counters in a ten-frame, touching each once.
  3. Write 6 and explain that the written numeral names how many counters are in the set.
  4. Show 0 with an empty frame and explain that zero means none.

Guided practice

  1. Take turns drawing a card, building its quantity, and writing the numeral.
  2. Include 0, a number from 1 through 9, and a teen number.
  3. Let the learner trace a large model before writing independently when needed.

Independent practice

  1. Complete five on-screen numeral-and-quantity questions without an answer-revealing hint.
  2. Build one selected quantity with counters to check the answer.

Templates: k.cc.a.3.object-count, k.cc.a.3.numeral-quantity, k.cc.a.3.numeral-name, k.cc.a.3.zero-quantity

Extension

  1. Draw a group of objects for a teen numeral, then trade papers with an adult to count and check.
  2. Write the numeral again without looking at the card.

Reteach

  1. Work with 0 through 5 only.
  2. Match each card to a prebuilt group before asking the learner to build a group.
  3. Offer a large numeral to trace, then copy beside it.

Mastery evidence

  • Matches numerals 0 through 20 to quantities in three different arrangements.
  • Writes selected numerals from 0 through 20 in a recognizable form; numeral reversals alone do not invalidate quantity understanding.
  • Answers at least four of five independent items correctly without an answer-revealing hint.

Day 5: Counting and Quantities Checkpoint

Objective: I can use counting order, count on, and match numerals to quantities in one mixed investigation.

Adult setup: Create three stations: a mixed numeral-card path, a bag of counters, and paper with a writing tool. Choose a quiet time and plan a movement break between stations.

Text recommendation: Reread the counting text used earlier in the sequence and invite the learner to notice a counting idea that now feels easier.

Warm-up

  1. Ask the learner to choose a favorite counting strategy from the prior days and demonstrate it.

Explicit model

  1. Model checking work by touching each object once, comparing the final count to the numeral, and correcting a mismatch without negative language.

Guided practice

  1. At station one, repair a number path with two missing cards.
  2. At station two, start at a chosen number and count on five.
  3. At station three, build and write a quantity selected by the learner.

Independent practice

  1. Complete six mixed on-screen questions, two for each standard, without an answer-revealing hint.
  2. Use counters or the number path if they help show the learner's thinking.

Templates: k.cc.a.1.count-from-given, k.cc.a.1.missing-in-order, k.cc.a.2.missing-next, k.cc.a.2.count-on-two, k.cc.a.3.numeral-quantity, k.cc.a.3.zero-quantity

Extension

  1. Create a new counting challenge for an adult using a starting number, a quantity, and a matching numeral.

Reteach

  1. Choose the one skill that needs more support and repeat its concrete model from Day 1, 2, 3, or 4.
  2. Reduce the range to 0 through 10 when the difficulty is counting on or matching quantities, then increase it only after a successful explanation.

Mastery evidence

  • Completes one concrete task for each standard and explains the counting action used.
  • Answers at least five of six mixed independent items correctly without an answer-revealing hint.
  • Record the evidence as lesson progress only; the application mastery policy still requires repeated independent evidence across sessions and templates.

Accessibility and accommodations

  • Read every direction aloud and allow the learner to replay it without penalty.
  • Use large, high-contrast numerals and manipulatives that can be distinguished by touch.
  • Accept pointing, eye gaze, spoken answers, or moving counters when writing is not the skill being observed.
  • Offer extra processing time and a reduced 0-through-10 range without treating the accommodation as failed evidence.

Audio supported: Yes; requires color: No; reduced-motion safe: Yes.

Putting Together and Taking Apart

k.math.operations-and-number-bonds · Grade K math · validated

A five-day, hands-on sequence for representing addition and subtraction, solving stories, decomposing numbers, making ten, and building fluency within five.

Standards

K.OA.A.1, K.OA.A.2, K.OA.A.3, K.OA.A.4, K.OA.A.5

Materials

  • Ten small counters in two visibly different groups (alternatives: buttons and beans, two colors of blocks, coins and small toys)
  • Two paper plates or work mats (alternatives: two sheets of paper, two shallow containers)
  • Numeral cards from 0 through 10 (alternatives: numbers written on scrap paper)
  • A ten-frame (alternatives: a hand-drawn grid with ten boxes)
  • Paper and a thick pencil or crayon (alternatives: a small whiteboard and marker)

Day 1: Show Putting Together and Taking Apart

Objective: I can use objects, drawings, and words to show addition and subtraction.

Adult setup: Place four counters on one mat and three on the other. Keep the remaining counters out of sight until the subtraction model.

Text recommendation: Choose a short picture book in which objects join a group or leave a group; pause before the result and act it out with counters.

Warm-up

  1. Count each group of counters separately.
  2. Move the groups together and count the combined group.

Explicit model

  1. Tell a joining story while moving two groups together and draw a quick picture of the action.
  2. Tell a taking-away story while moving counters away and cross out the counters that left.
  3. Name addition as putting together and subtraction as taking apart or taking away.

Guided practice

  1. Invite the learner to act out one joining story and one taking-away story.
  2. Ask the learner to explain what changed before counting the result.
  3. Match each story to a simple drawing together.

Independent practice

  1. Complete four on-screen joining and taking-away story items.
  2. Use counters if needed, but let the learner decide whether the story joins or separates groups.

Templates: k.oa.a.1.represent-operations, k.oa.a.2.word-problems

Extension

  1. Create a new story for five counters and ask an adult to act it out.

Reteach

  1. Use no more than three counters.
  2. Say show me together or show me take away before adding numbers to the story.
  3. Repeat the same action with a drawing after the objects are clear.

Mastery evidence

  • Represents one addition and one subtraction situation with objects or drawings.
  • Explains whether a story puts together or takes apart before finding the result.

Day 2: Solve Number Stories

Objective: I can solve addition and subtraction stories within ten.

Adult setup: Prepare ten counters and two mats. Write simple story starters such as had, got more, gave away, and left.

Text recommendation: Use a familiar story or daily event with a small number of objects, such as setting out snacks or putting toys away.

Warm-up

  1. Act out two plus one with counters.
  2. Act out three minus one with the same counters.

Explicit model

  1. Read a story once without moving anything and name what is known.
  2. Read it again while modeling the action with counters.
  3. Count the result and check that the answer makes sense in the story.

Guided practice

  1. Solve one joining story and one separating story together.
  2. Have the learner choose the action and the adult record a picture or equation.
  3. Retell each story using the final answer.

Independent practice

  1. Complete five on-screen word problems.
  2. After each answer, briefly say what happened to the group.

Templates: k.oa.a.1.represent-operations, k.oa.a.2.word-problems

Extension

  1. Make two different stories that both have the answer five.

Reteach

  1. Shorten the story to one sentence.
  2. Place the starting counters before reading the action word.
  3. Ask only did the group grow or get smaller before solving.

Mastery evidence

  • Solves at least one addition and one subtraction story using a self-chosen representation.
  • Connects the numerical answer back to the objects in the story.

Day 3: Break Numbers Into Parts

Objective: I can show a number as two smaller parts in more than one way.

Adult setup: Place six counters where the learner can reach them. Put one paper plate on each side of the work area.

Text recommendation: Choose a counting book with groups that can be rearranged, then notice that moving objects does not change how many there are.

Warm-up

  1. Count five counters.
  2. Move some counters to each plate and count the total again.

Explicit model

  1. Split five into one and four, then record 1 + 4.
  2. Recombine the counters and split five into two and three.
  3. Explain that both pairs are parts of the same whole.

Guided practice

  1. Choose a whole from four through eight.
  2. Find two different ways to split it between the plates.
  3. Record each split with a drawing or number cards and verify by recombining.

Independent practice

  1. Complete four on-screen items about joining groups and pairs that make a whole.
  2. Use the plates only if a mental image is not enough.

Templates: k.oa.a.1.represent-operations, k.oa.a.3.decompose-numbers

Extension

  1. Find every two-part split for six and arrange the recordings in order.

Reteach

  1. Use a whole of three or four.
  2. Keep one part fixed and move one counter at a time to the other plate.
  3. Recount the whole after every move.

Mastery evidence

  • Decomposes a number within ten into two parts in at least two ways.
  • Recombines the parts and confirms that the whole is unchanged.

Day 4: Find the Part That Makes Ten

Objective: I can find the number that goes with another number to make ten.

Adult setup: Place a ten-frame and ten counters on the table. Prepare numeral cards 0 through 10.

Text recommendation: Use a counting text organized in groups of ten and notice how many spaces or objects complete each group.

Warm-up

  1. Fill one row of the ten-frame and count five.
  2. Fill the second row and count ten.

Explicit model

  1. Place six counters in the ten-frame and count the four empty spaces.
  2. State that six and four make ten, then verify by filling the spaces.
  3. Record the relationship with cards, a drawing, or 6 + 4 = 10.

Guided practice

  1. Build three different starting numbers in the ten-frame.
  2. Predict the missing part, fill the spaces, and recount ten.
  3. Compare two combinations and notice that every completed frame has the same whole.

Independent practice

  1. Complete five on-screen number-part and make-ten items.
  2. Try the final two items without placing counters first.

Templates: k.oa.a.3.decompose-numbers, k.oa.a.4.make-ten

Extension

  1. Make a set of matching cards for all pairs that make ten.

Reteach

  1. Keep the ten-frame visible and use a starting number of eight or nine.
  2. Count empty spaces one at a time before naming the missing part.
  3. Repeat with one fewer starting counter.

Mastery evidence

  • Finds the missing part for at least four different make-ten combinations.
  • Explains that the filled spaces and empty spaces together total ten.

Day 5: Use What You Know Within Five

Objective: I can solve addition and subtraction within five accurately and explain a strategy.

Adult setup: Prepare five counters, numeral cards 0 through 5, and the two mats. Plan a brief movement break before the mixed independent practice.

Text recommendation: Revisit a favorite joining or taking-away story from the week and invite the learner to explain the mathematics in the pictures.

Warm-up

  1. Show each number from zero through five with fingers or counters.
  2. Say one more and one less for several starting numbers.

Explicit model

  1. Solve three plus two by recognizing parts that make five.
  2. Check the answer with counters, then solve five minus two by removing one known part.
  3. Explain that accuracy matters more than speed.

Guided practice

  1. Mix joining, separating, missing-part, and make-five examples.
  2. Ask the learner to choose counters, fingers, a drawing, or a known fact.
  3. Discuss why the chosen strategy fits each example.

Independent practice

  1. Complete six mixed on-screen operations items within the taught range.
  2. After the final item, explain one answer using objects, a drawing, or words.

Templates: k.oa.a.1.represent-operations, k.oa.a.2.word-problems, k.oa.a.3.decompose-numbers, k.oa.a.4.make-ten, k.oa.a.5.fluency-within-five

Extension

  1. Invent a small number story, record it with a picture, and ask an adult to solve it.

Reteach

  1. Return to one operation and a maximum whole of three.
  2. Model the action, then have the learner copy it with a new example.
  3. Increase the whole only after two accurate explanations.

Mastery evidence

  • Solves a varied set of addition and subtraction situations within five accurately.
  • Explains at least one solution using a representation or relationship rather than an unsupported guess.
  • Treat speed as observational information, not a requirement for success.

Accessibility and accommodations

  • Read every story aloud and permit replay without penalty.
  • Use tactile or larger counters and high-contrast mats when helpful.
  • Accept pointing, moving objects, eye gaze, signing, drawing, or speech when handwriting is not the target.
  • Reduce the total quantity while preserving the operation when working-memory or motor demands interfere.

Audio supported: Yes; requires color: No; reduced-motion safe: Yes.

Shapes, Measurement, and Data

k.math.shapes-measurement-and-data · Grade K math · validated

A five-day hands-on sequence for describing positions and shapes, comparing and composing shapes, measuring attributes directly, and sorting and counting objects.

Standards

K.G.A.1, K.G.A.2, K.G.A.3, K.G.B.4, K.G.B.5, K.G.B.6, K.MD.A.1, K.MD.A.2, K.MD.B.3

Materials

  • Flat shape cutouts in varied sizes and orientations (alternatives: hand-drawn paper shapes, household objects with traceable faces)
  • Solid objects such as a ball, box, can, and cone (alternatives: building blocks, safe food containers)
  • Building sticks and modeling dough (alternatives: straws and reusable adhesive, craft sticks and string)
  • Objects to compare and sort (alternatives: socks, spoons, or blocks, paper picture cards)
  • Paper and a thick pencil or crayon (alternatives: a small whiteboard and marker)

Day 1: Shapes Around and Beside Us

Objective: I can name a flat shape and describe where it is.

Adult setup: Place circles, triangles, rectangles, squares, and hexagons in varied sizes and rotations around a small play area. Do not organize or identify shapes by color because color is not the defining attribute.

Text recommendation: Choose a shape-search picture book that shows shapes in multiple orientations and everyday settings.

Warm-up

  1. Use a toy to act out above, below, beside, in front of, and behind.
  2. Let the learner copy each position and say or point to the matching word.

Explicit model

  1. Name a triangle by its three straight sides and corners even when it points sideways.
  2. Describe its location using a complete position phrase, such as beside the box.
  3. Rotate the triangle and explain why its name does not change.

Guided practice

  1. Find and name shapes in several positions around the play area.
  2. Ask the learner to place a named shape above, beside, or behind an object.
  3. Compare a large and small example to confirm size does not change the shape name.

Independent practice

  1. Complete four on-screen position and shape-name checks.
  2. Then follow one adult direction that combines a shape and a position without a visual answer choice.

Templates: k.g.a.1.positions-and-shapes, k.g.a.2.name-shapes

Extension

  1. Give an adult two shape-and-position directions to follow, then check the result.

Reteach

  1. Use one shape and two contrasting positions at a time.
  2. Trace the shape's sides and corners before naming it, then physically move it to each position.

Mastery evidence

  • Names common flat shapes across more than one size or orientation.
  • Accurately follows or gives at least two directions using position words.

Day 2: Flat Shapes and Solid Shapes

Objective: I can tell whether a shape is flat or solid and compare its parts.

Adult setup: Pair flat shape cutouts with safe solid objects, such as a circle and ball or rectangle and box. Include examples that differ in color, size, and orientation.

Text recommendation: Choose an illustrated book about building or objects in a town and notice flat faces and solid forms in the pictures and environment.

Warm-up

  1. Review two flat shapes by tracing their boundaries.
  2. Roll a ball and slide a box, then describe what happened.

Explicit model

  1. Model that a paper circle is flat while a ball is solid and can be held from different sides.
  2. Compare a square and rectangle by counting sides and corners and noticing what is alike and different.
  3. Turn each example to show that orientation does not change its attributes.

Guided practice

  1. Sort objects into flat-shape pictures and solid objects using touch and sight.
  2. Compare two flat shapes by sides and corners.
  3. Compare two solid objects by whether they roll, stack, or have flat faces without using behavior as the only definition.

Independent practice

  1. Complete four on-screen shape-name, flat-or-solid, and shape-comparison checks.
  2. Explain one answer using a side, corner, face, or solid-versus-flat attribute.

Templates: k.g.a.2.name-shapes, k.g.a.3.flat-and-solid, k.g.b.4.compare-shapes

Extension

  1. Find one solid household object and sketch one of its flat faces.

Reteach

  1. Contrast one flat cutout with one solid object the learner can handle.
  2. Trace around the flat shape, then turn the solid object and feel more than one face or surface.

Mastery evidence

  • Classifies familiar examples as flat shapes or solid shapes.
  • Compares two shapes using at least one defining attribute rather than color or size alone.

Day 3: Build and Compose Shapes

Objective: I can build a shape and combine smaller shapes to make a larger shape.

Adult setup: Prepare building sticks, dough connectors, shape cutouts, and a clear workspace. Create one model triangle and one picture made from two or more shapes.

Text recommendation: Choose a picture book with buildings, quilts, mosaics, or tangram-like illustrations that invite noticing shapes within larger pictures.

Warm-up

  1. Trace and count the sides of a triangle and rectangle.
  2. Choose which sticks might build each outline.

Explicit model

  1. Build a triangle from three sticks and connect each endpoint.
  2. Join two triangles to make a larger shape and trace the new outside boundary.
  3. Name both the small component shapes and the composed shape when it has a familiar name.

Guided practice

  1. Build a square or rectangle from sticks and check its sides and corners.
  2. Combine two or more cutouts to match a simple silhouette.
  3. Separate the composition and find a different arrangement using the same pieces.

Independent practice

  1. Complete five on-screen comparison, building, and composing checks.
  2. Build or draw one named shape and make one larger picture from at least two shapes without copying the adult model exactly.

Templates: k.g.b.4.compare-shapes, k.g.b.5.build-shapes, k.g.b.6.compose-shapes

Extension

  1. Design a shape creature or building and tell which shapes were combined.

Reteach

  1. Place sticks directly over a printed outline before building without the outline.
  2. Use only two large cutouts and rotate one at a time until their edges meet.

Mastery evidence

  • Builds or draws a recognizable flat shape from its attributes.
  • Combines at least two simple shapes and identifies the parts within the composition.

Day 4: Describe and Compare Measurements

Objective: I can describe measurable attributes and directly compare two objects.

Adult setup: Choose pairs of objects that can be aligned for length, lifted safely for weight, or visibly compared for capacity. Avoid pairs whose difference is too subtle to judge directly.

Text recommendation: Choose a read-aloud about size, growth, cooking, or construction and notice words such as longer, shorter, heavier, lighter, and holds more.

Warm-up

  1. Name two measurable attributes of a familiar object, such as length and weight.
  2. Act out long and short with arm movements without assigning numbers.

Explicit model

  1. Align two pencils at one endpoint before deciding which is longer.
  2. Compare two safe objects by holding one in each hand and describing which feels heavier.
  3. Explain that fair comparison uses the same attribute and a direct method.

Guided practice

  1. Compare three object pairs for length after aligning their endpoints.
  2. Compare one pair for weight and one pair for capacity using safe direct methods.
  3. Ask the learner to state the attribute and comparison word for each result.

Independent practice

  1. Complete five on-screen attribute and direct-comparison checks.
  2. Choose two new objects, select one shared attribute, compare them directly, and explain the result.

Templates: k.md.a.1.measurable-attributes, k.md.a.2.compare-measurement

Extension

  1. Order three objects from shortest to longest by repeating pairwise direct comparisons.

Reteach

  1. Compare only length with two clearly different objects.
  2. Align endpoints together and trace from the shared start to each endpoint before using longer or shorter.

Mastery evidence

  • Names at least one measurable attribute for a familiar object.
  • Directly compares two objects sharing that attribute and uses an accurate comparison word.

Day 5: Sort, Count, and Describe Data

Objective: I can sort objects into categories, count each group, and compare the groups.

Adult setup: Prepare ten to fifteen objects with at least two observable sorting attributes. Make category labels with words and simple symbols so categories do not depend on color alone.

Text recommendation: Choose an information book with pictured categories, such as animals, foods, or vehicles, and discuss how the author groups information.

Warm-up

  1. Describe two attributes of one object.
  2. Sort three sample objects into two adult-named categories and explain the rule.

Explicit model

  1. Choose one clear sorting rule and place every object into exactly one category.
  2. Count each category by touching each object once and record the totals with numerals or marks.
  3. Compare the category counts using more, fewer, or the same number.

Guided practice

  1. Sort a mixed collection together and let the learner name the category rule.
  2. Recount after arranging each category in a line to check accuracy.
  3. Resort the same objects by a different attribute and discuss how the data changed.

Independent practice

  1. Complete six on-screen attribute, comparison, and sorting-and-counting checks.
  2. Independently sort a small set, count each category, and state which has more, fewer, or the same number.

Templates: k.md.a.1.measurable-attributes, k.md.a.2.compare-measurement, k.md.b.3.sort-and-count

Extension

  1. Draw a simple picture record with one mark per object in each category and ask an adult a comparison question.

Reteach

  1. Use six objects and two categories with an obvious rule.
  2. Place category symbols above two rows, touch-count each row, and pair objects across rows before comparing totals.

Mastery evidence

  • Classifies a collection into named categories using a consistent rule.
  • Counts each category accurately and compares the category totals with appropriate language.
  • Explains that a new sorting attribute can organize the same objects differently.

Accessibility and accommodations

  • Use spoken and visible labels for position, shape, attribute, and category words.
  • Provide large tactile shapes and safe easy-grip objects when helpful.
  • Accept pointing, eye gaze, object placement, signing, or spoken explanation when drawing or handwriting is not assessed.
  • Use size, texture, symbol, or position in addition to color so no task requires color discrimination.
